# story.md: from an idea to a story worth sending

Used by stages 02_ideas and 03_script. Tags: [S] sourced (see `toolbox.md` → Sources), [O] opinion.

## What the platform rewards
- Instagram's top ranking signals: watch time, likes, **sends**. Sends weigh most for reaching non-followers (Mosseri, Jan 2025) [S]. New clients come from reels people forward. Every idea answers: **who sends this, to whom, with what message?** ("this is you", "we're doing this", "look at her face").
- Originality is ranked; reposts and aggregated content lose reach [S]. Trial Reels test a version on non-followers first [S].
- TikTok's own rule: "Don't make ads, make TikToks". Be vulnerable, be weird, be in the process [S].

## Where good ideas come from (ranked for a solo craftsperson)
1. **DM and consult mining.** Every repeated client question, fear or odd request is a premise. Keep the exact phrasings: specificity is the joke and the hook [S: tattooer Jake Karamol, Borrowed Time Tattoo, barber Sly Huncho].
2. **Pre-session story scouting.** Read the booking and plan 2–3 questions to ask mid-session ("what's this one for?"). The client's answer is the story [S].
3. **Recurring series.** One format with the same title (ep 1, 2, 3), so the viewer who liked #1 gets #2, and the blank page disappears [S: Jenny Hoyos].
4. **The artist's persona.** The accounts that grew are built on the artist's voice: deadpan, warm, self-ironic. Personality beats process; plain before/afters are what everyone posts [S].
5. **Reference reels.** Take the *mechanism* of a reel that works (see `formats.md`, `examples/`), never its content. Our twist must be true and ours.
6. **The 4 A's as a mix check**: actionable (aftercare), analytical (numbers: "45 hours"), aspirational (a client's story), anthropological (fear of pain, commitment, regret; the most sendable) [S].

## The engine: conflict, not "and then"
- A short is a mini-movie: set up a conflict, resolve it, open a new one. Beats join with **"but" / "so"**, never "and then" [S: Hoyos, Kallaway]. A process list (look → mark → pierce → reveal) is "and then": no stakes, no soul.
- Write the **hook and the last line first**, then the middle [S].
- **Peak-end**: viewers remember the best moment and the ending. Plan one sharp peak and a fast ending. Foreshadow the peak early (open on the laugh or the tears) [S].
- **One target emotion**, chosen before writing: laugh, awe, warmth, recognition, curiosity. The edit must transfer it [S: Kallaway].
- It must work **on mute**: picture + text alone tell it [S].

## Story shapes for 7–30 s (detail and footage per shape in `formats.md`)
| Shape | Why it works |
|---|---|
| Sketch game: setup → escalation → most extreme beat | one odd behaviour, heightened each beat [S] |
| "Things clients say" / POV | recognition; viewers tag the friend who said it [O] |
| Expectation vs reality | the gap is the joke; the truth is the payoff [O] |
| Challenge to the viewer ("4 drawings to convince you") | direct address + stakes + an ask; comments answer it |
| Then vs now (2021 → 2026) | growth arc in one frame; the payoff is scale or skill |
| Client reaction arc | the peak is a face, not the work [S] |
| One person's meaning (mini-doc) | the why is universal; built from the client's own words [S] |
| Effort contrast ("15 s for you, 45 h for me") | awe at the hours; one continuous reveal |
| Before/after **with stakes** | "she hid it 8 years, BUT the ink was too dark, SO…" [S] |

## Humour that isn't cringe
- One game, heightened, ending on its most extreme beat [S]. Specific beats generic: use the exact words [S].
- Reaction shots and deadpan carry it; hold the reaction about 1 s longer than feels natural [O].
- Laugh at the situation or yourself, **never at a client** [S]. A real client as the butt of a joke can go viral the wrong way. Acted or composite clients are safer [S].
- Keep one consistent persona [S]. Cringe tells: an explained joke, a sales line inside the punchline, a trend copied without our twist, lines the artist can't say naturally [O].

## Kill checklist (any "no" = rework or kill)
1. Name who sends it and to whom.
2. The premise fits one sentence with one game or one turn.
3. There is a "but" (conflict, turn, stakes).
4. It works on mute.
5. One clear peak; a fast ending.
6. Only this artist could post it (her voice, her clients, her studio). If any studio could post it, kill it.
7. Nobody is the butt of the joke.
8. The ask sits after the payoff, outside the joke.
9. It is true: real people, real order, real words.

## Soul vs generic: observable traits
A person with a voice on screen or in VO · one specific human detail · something at stake · the emotion lands on a face · imperfect, real footage · the structure differs from the last post · the ending means something (a line, a look, a callback), not only a logo and a CTA. Time goes into the idea: Hoyos spends 10–20 h per short, mostly on ideas [S].
