# shooting.md: footage that carries a person, not only the work

Used by 04_footage. Editing can't add a face, a voice or a reaction that wasn't filmed.
The filmer is a second person; the artist never handles the phone and never pauses for it.

## The three must-haves of a client story
**The why** (their own words) · **the face before** (nerves, excitement) · **the face at the reveal**.
With these, a story edit exists. With process clips only, you have a timelapse [O].

## Coverage for one session [S: Videomaker five shots, applied]
| Beat | Shot | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| Arrival | wide of the room with the client entering or sitting, 5 s | must |
| Face before | eye-level close-up: breathing, laughing, looking at the stencil | **must** |
| The why | one-line answer, face, lav mic | **must** |
| Stencil moment | the client's face seeing it in the mirror | must |
| First touch | needle on skin + the client's face (two clips or one over-the-shoulder) | must |
| Process | 3–5 inserts: hands, ink cap, wipe, linework | must |
| Conversation | 20–60 s of them talking and laughing | **must** |
| Artist's face | concentration close-up | nice |
| Reveal | mirror pre-framed on the client's face; face first, then the piece | **must** |
| After | hug, photo together, "one sentence: what do you see?" | must |
| Anchor wide | one locked wide angle, repeated at each stage (shows growth) | for big pieces |
| B-roll + room tone | buzz close-up, the door, light; 30 s room tone | must (free) |

## Getting a real answer from a non-actor in 30 s [S + O]
- Consent off camera first ("you'll see it first, you can say no"), then a yes on camera.
- The filmer stands beside the artist's eyeline, phone at the client's eye level, 1–1.5 m. The client talks to the filmer, not the lens.
- Ask after the stencil (anticipation), in a quiet stretch (relaxed), right after the reveal (peak).
- One short question at a time: "What's this one for?" · "Why now?" · "Worse or easier than you thought?" · "One sentence: what do you see?"
- Then: "Say it once more as if I'd never heard it." Stay silent 2 s after they finish.
- Never script lines, feed lines or ask for a second reaction. The first look happens once.

## Sound [S]
- Speech early lifts retention (see `hooks.md`). Lav on the **client's** chest, 15–20 cm below the mouth, on the side away from the tattoo; foam against rustle; 10 s test clip.
- 32-bit float backup recording (e.g. DJI Mic 3) saves clipped laughs.
- Studio music off during the "why"; music under speech can't be removed.
- One clean 10 s buzz-on-skin close-up as a sound effect; 30 s room tone.

## Phone settings [S]
4K · 30 fps for speech, 60 fps for action (slow-mo / ramps), 120 only for a planned moment · **HDR Video off** · **Auto FPS off** · **Lock White Balance on** · AE/AF lock on skin, exposure −0.3 to −0.7 so fresh ink doesn't clip · 1× lens, wiped · window or bounced LED light, no ring light in the client's eyes · vertical for faces · Do Not Disturb · start 2 s before, stop 2 s after, hold statics 10 s.

## Shots that make the edit exciting later [S + O]
- Hand or towel over the lens to end one shot and start the next.
- Whip pans in the same direction on both sides of a cut.
- Two angles of a repeated action (wipe, ink dip) to cut on the movement.
- Entrances into and exits out of an empty frame.
- 60/120 fps moments for ramps: the stencil peel, the first wipe, the wrap, the turn to the mirror, the hug.
- Eye-level for people, top-down for the work. Most archives are all top-down: people first.

## Planned skits and premises
A skit is shot like a scene: a locked wide, the lines said 2–3 times, a reaction close-up per line, and room tone. Write the lines as the person would say them; let them reword.
